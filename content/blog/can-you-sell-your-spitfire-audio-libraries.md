Spitfire Audio libraries are all over film, TV and game music: BBC Symphony Orchestra, Abbey Road One, Albion, the Originals, and many more. They're not cheap, so it's natural to wonder whether you can sell one you no longer use, or buy one second-hand.

Here's what Spitfire's own license says.

## Can you resell a Spitfire Audio library?

**No.** Spitfire's End User Licence Agreement grants you a **personal, non-transferable licence**. Section 6 of the EULA, titled **"No Resale of Products"**, says products must not be given or transferred to any third party, or offered for resale.

The EULA also says that when you buy a product, you're buying a **limited licence for use, not ownership**, and that you may not rent, lease, sell, sublicense, distribute or transfer it, except as the agreement allows.

That's why Spitfire libraries can't be listed on Plugin Resale: we only list licenses the developer will move to the buyer.

## It's not just the license: the samples too

Spitfire's EULA is also strict about the sounds themselves. You can use the samples inside your own music, but reselling or distributing them as sounds, loops or sample packs, even edited or reprocessed, is forbidden. That's a separate rule from license resale, but it's worth knowing if you work with Spitfire libraries.

## What about companies?

If you bought a library as a company, Spitfire says the licence is granted **only to the individual person** who registered or made the purchase. It doesn't extend to other directors, owners or employees. So even inside a studio, a Spitfire library doesn't pass from one person to another.

## Why a "used Spitfire library" is a risky buy

Since Spitfire doesn't transfer licenses, a seller can't put a library in your name. What's usually on offer is:

- **A shared account login.** The seller keeps control, and Spitfire's EULA says you must not let anyone else use your email or password.
- **Downloaded files.** Without a license in your own account, you have no right to use them.

In both cases, you pay for something you don't own.

## What about EU law?

In the EU, the law may allow the resale of software bought outright even when a license forbids it. But Spitfire won't move the license to the buyer, so the buyer would have to go to court to use it.
