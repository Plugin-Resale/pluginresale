Cubase has been around for decades, and so have its owners. Many engineers have moved from Cubase Artist to Pro, switched to Nuendo, or left for another DAW, and still have a Steinberg license sitting in their account. The same goes for WaveLab, Dorico, HALion or SpectraLayers.

Steinberg allows resale, and publishes a detailed guide for it. How it works depends on which licensing system your software uses.

## Can you resell a Steinberg license?

Yes. Steinberg has an official process for selling or giving away used Steinberg software. It keeps the right to **refuse the ownership transfer if it suspects a violation of the license agreement**.

What can't be sold:

- **Educational licenses** are personal and must not be passed on or sold.
- **OEM software** bundled with hardware, like Cubase AI, Cubase LE, WaveLab LE or Sequel LE, must not be sold separately from the hardware it came with. That also applies to included effect plugins, like the Basic FX Suite collection.

## Which licensing system is yours?

**Steinberg Licensing (current products, since January 2022).** All current products use it, and licenses are managed in the **Steinberg Activation Manager**. This is the case for current versions like Cubase 15, Nuendo 15 or Dorico 6.

**USB-eLicenser (older software).** The license is stored on a USB dongle.

**Soft-eLicenser (older software).** The license is stored in a virtual container on your hard disk.

**Important:** Steinberg shut down the eLicenser service on **20 May 2025**. Since then:

- Licenses on a **USB-eLicenser** can no longer be changed, so several licenses stored on the same dongle can **only be transferred together**.
- Licenses in a **Soft-eLicenser** can no longer be moved at all. Steinberg says this makes it practically impossible to sell such a license without handing over the whole computer.

## How to transfer a Steinberg Licensing license, step by step

1. **Agree on the deal and get paid.** On Plugin Resale, the buyer pays you directly with PayPal Goods & Services.
2. **Deactivate the license on every computer** in Steinberg Activation Manager. A transfer isn't possible while the license is still active somewhere (it can be used on up to three computers).
3. **Log in to your Steinberg account**, go to **Support** and click **Create support request**.
4. **Choose "License transfer — request change of ownership"**.
5. **Include the name of the license** as shown in your account, ideally with a screenshot. The details must match your Steinberg account, or the request won't be processed.
6. **Steinberg verifies it** and sends a **new Download Access Code** by email. Steinberg says this may take **several business days**.
7. **Give the buyer the unused Download Access Code.** They download the license with the Steinberg Download Assistant.

## Selling on a USB-eLicenser

If the dongle isn't registered to a Steinberg account, you can simply hand it over, and the buyer registers it. If it is registered to yours, send the same change-of-ownership request with the dongle's serial number. Remember: everything on that dongle goes together.

## Traps to avoid

**Selling a Soft-eLicenser license.** Since the shutdown, it can't be moved.

**Selling only part of a USB-eLicenser.** Everything on the dongle goes with it.

**Selling OEM or educational licenses.** Cubase AI/LE with your interface, or an education version, can't be sold separately.

**Missing information in your request.** The details must match your account exactly. Add a screenshot.

## How to price a used Steinberg license

Start from what the buyer would pay for it new today. A used license has to be clearly cheaper than the best new price they can find, or they'll just buy new.

Then adjust:

- **Edition and version:** Pro, Artist or Elements, and the version number. Older versions are worth less, since the buyer may pay to update.
- **Licensing system:** a current Steinberg Licensing license is easier to transfer than an older dongle.
- **Delay:** say that Steinberg takes several business days.
