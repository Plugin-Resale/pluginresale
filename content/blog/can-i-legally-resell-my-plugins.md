Most of us have a folder full of plugins we bought on sale, tried for a week and never opened again. Selling them second-hand sounds obvious, but a lot of producers hesitate: the license agreement you clicked through probably says something like "non-transferable", and nobody wants to get their account banned.

The short answer: **in most cases, yes, you can resell a plugin license, as long as the developer's transfer process is followed and you stop using it yourself.** The longer answer depends on where you live, what kind of license you have and which developer made the plugin.

## You don't own the plugin, you own a license

When you buy a plugin, you don't buy the software itself. You buy a *license*: the right to install and use it under the developer's terms (the EULA). That license is usually tied to something the developer controls: a user account, a serial number, an iLok account or a machine authorization.

That's why reselling a plugin is different from reselling a hardware compressor. Even when the law allows the sale, the buyer can only use the plugin if the license is moved to their name, and that almost always goes through the developer.

## In the EU: the UsedSoft ruling

In 2012, the Court of Justice of the European Union ruled in *UsedSoft v Oracle* (case C-128/11) that the resale of downloaded software licenses is lawful under certain conditions. Once a developer has sold a copy of its software in the EU, it can't use its copyright to stop that copy from being resold, **even if the license agreement forbids transfers**.

The conditions matter:

- **The license must be perpetual and paid for.** The ruling covers software sold for a one-off price with no time limit. Subscriptions and rentals are not covered.
- **You must stop using it.** The seller has to make their own copy unusable at the time of the sale: uninstall it and deactivate every authorization.
- **You sell the license as a whole.** You can't keep one seat and sell another if they came as a single license, or sell a license for more users than you bought.

The ruling applies to software. Sample libraries are a grey area: a later ruling on e-books (*Tom Kabinet*, 2019) held that digital *content* is not covered by the same rule, and a library of samples sits somewhere between content and software. For libraries, the developer's own policy is what counts in practice.

The UK followed the UsedSoft ruling while it was in the EU. It remains part of UK law for now, but the higher UK courts are now allowed to depart from it.

## In the US and elsewhere: the license agreement decides

US law is less favourable to resellers. In *Vernor v. Autodesk* (2010), a US federal appeals court held that when a software agreement says you are a licensee and significantly restricts transfers, you are not the owner of your copy, so the "first sale" rule that lets you resell a book or a CD doesn't apply.

In practice, that means the developer's license agreement and transfer policy decide whether you can resell. Many other countries work the same way.

## What developers actually allow

Whatever the law says, the part that matters day to day is the developer's transfer policy, because the developer is the one who moves the license to the buyer's account. Policies vary a lot:

- **Free, self-service transfers** from your user account in a couple of clicks.
- **Transfers with a fee**, paid by the seller or the buyer, sometimes a flat amount, sometimes a percentage of the plugin's price.
- **Transfers by request**, where you email support with both parties' details and wait for them to process it.
- **No transfers at all**, written in the license agreement.
- **Restrictions**: some developers only allow one transfer per license, or only between buyers and sellers in certain countries.

## Licenses you usually can't resell

Even with a developer that allows transfers, some licenses are almost always excluded:

- **NFR licenses** (Not For Resale), given to reviewers, beta testers and artists.
- **Educational and academic licenses**, sold at a discount to students and teachers.
- **Bundled and OEM licenses**, that came free with an audio interface, a controller, a magazine or another product.
- **Subscriptions and rent-to-own plans** that aren't fully paid off.
- **Upgrades and crossgrades**, which often can only be transferred together with the license they upgraded.
- **Single plugins taken out of a bundle**: many developers only let you transfer the whole bundle.

If you're not sure what kind of license you have, look at your account on the developer's website or at your original order confirmation.

## How to resell a plugin the right way

1. **Check the developer's transfer policy.** Is it transferable, is there a fee, who pays it, how long does it take?
2. **Check your license type.** Make sure it's a full, perpetual, paid license, not NFR, educational or bundled.
3. **Agree on the price and the fee** with the buyer before starting, so nobody gets a surprise.
4. **Get paid with a protected payment method.** On Plugin Resale, buyers pay sellers directly with PayPal Goods & Services (never Friends & Family), so both sides are covered by PayPal's protection.
5. **Uninstall the plugin and deactivate every authorization** on your computers and your iLok.
6. **Start the official transfer** with the developer, and keep the confirmation emails until the buyer has the license in their account.
