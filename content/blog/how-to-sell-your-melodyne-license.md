Melodyne is one of those tools engineers upgrade over the years: Essential first, then Assistant or Editor, then Studio. Along the way, some people end up with a license they no longer need, or switch to another pitch tool altogether.

Celemony allows Melodyne licenses to change hands. Here's how it works, according to our database and Celemony's help center.

## Can you resell a Melodyne license?

Yes. Celemony lets you transfer a Melodyne license to a new owner, with a few exceptions:

- **Serial numbers already used for an upgrade** can't be sold. If you upgraded from Assistant to Studio, the Assistant serial went into that upgrade. You sell the Studio license, not both.
- **Serials used as a coupon** can't be sold. Our database mentions Melodyne essential here.
- **Parts of the old Melodyne Studio 3 bundle** can't be sold separately.

## How much does it cost?

Our database doesn't list a transfer fee for Celemony. Ask Celemony if in doubt, before you agree on a price.

## How to transfer a Melodyne license, step by step

1. **Agree on the deal and get paid.** On Plugin Resale, the buyer pays you directly with PayPal Goods & Services.
2. **Deactivate Melodyne completely**, on every computer where it's activated.
3. **Write to Celemony support** to confirm the transfer. Include:
   - **Your details:** name, email and serial number.
   - **The buyer's details:** full name, email and postal address.
4. **Celemony moves the license** to the new owner.
5. **Uninstall Melodyne** on your side.

Our database doesn't list a typical delay. It depends on Celemony support.

## Traps to avoid

**Selling a serial you used to upgrade.** Only the current, upgraded license can be sold.

**Forgetting a computer.** The license has to be fully deactivated before the transfer.

**Missing the buyer's postal address.** Celemony asks for it. Collect it with the buyer's name and email before you write in.

**Selling an essential serial used as a coupon.** It can't be resold.

## How to price a used Melodyne license

Start from what the buyer would pay for it new today. A used license has to be clearly cheaper than the best new price they can find, or they'll just buy new.

Then adjust:

- **Edition matters a lot.** Studio, Editor, Assistant and Essential are very different products. Name the exact edition and version (Melodyne 5, for example).
- **Upgrade paths:** a buyer who plans to upgrade later will want to know exactly which edition they're starting from.
