Selling a plugin is easy. Getting the license into the buyer's name is the part that trips people up. There is no universal "transfer" button: each developer decides whether licenses can change hands, how, when and for how much.

The good news is that almost every developer uses one of five systems. Once you know which one you're dealing with, the rest is paperwork.

## Why a transfer is needed at all

A plugin license is registered to a person: a user account on the developer's website, an iLok account, or a serial number tied to your name. If you just hand over your serial number or your login, the license is still yours on paper. The developer can revoke it, the buyer can't get support or updates, and sharing an account is usually against the license terms.

An official transfer moves the license to the buyer's own account. It's the only way the buyer really owns what they paid for.

## The five transfer systems

### 1. Self-service from your account

The simplest case. You open your account on the developer's website, click "transfer", enter the buyer's details or get a code, and it's done in minutes.

- **Ableton** lets you transfer a Live license from your account for free; the buyer accepts it from theirs.
- **Native Instruments** gives you a Transfer ID that the buyer imports into their NI account ([full guide](/blog/how-to-transfer-a-native-instruments-license)).
- **FabFilter** works the same way, but you first buy a "License Transfer" in your account (about €10).

### 2. A request to support

Many developers handle transfers by hand. You open a support ticket or fill in a contact form with the buyer's name and email, and the developer moves the license.

- **iZotope**, **Arturia**, **u-he** and **Bitwig** all work this way.
- Expect anything from a few hours to a few business days.

### 3. A transfer form with a fee

Some developers have a dedicated form and charge a fee, often a percentage of the product's price. **Waves** is the best-known example: you deactivate the license, fill in the transfer form and pay through a link Waves sends you ([full guide](/blog/how-to-transfer-a-waves-license)).

### 4. iLok transfers

If a plugin is protected by iLok, the transfer usually happens in iLok License Manager: you pick the license and send it to the buyer's iLok User ID. PACE, the company behind iLok, charges its own fee, currently $25 per license or $50 for several in one go. **Softube** and **Slate Digital** use this route. The details and pitfalls are in our [iLok transfer guide](/blog/ilok-license-transfer-explained).

### 5. Hardware-bound licenses

A few licenses can only move together with a piece of hardware. **Universal Audio** UAD plug-ins, for example, follow the Apollo, Satellite or UAD-2 unit they are registered to: you sell the interface with its plug-ins, or you don't sell the plug-ins.

## What can block a transfer

Even with a developer that allows transfers, these are the usual reasons a request gets refused:

- **The license is too recent.** Many developers want you to own it for a while first: 30 days, 90 days, sometimes 6 months.
- **The license type isn't eligible.** NFR, educational, bundled with hardware, subscription or rent-to-own licenses are usually excluded. See [which licenses you can't resell](/blog/nfr-educational-bundled-licenses-resale).
- **You're splitting a bundle.** Many developers only let you transfer a bundle as a whole.
- **It was already transferred once.** Some developers allow a single transfer per license, or charge more for a second-hand license.
- **Some conditions aren't met.** Waves, for instance, requires an active Waves Update Plan.

## Checklist for sellers

1. Look up your developer on our [transfer rules database](/developers): transferable or not, fee, who pays, process.
2. Check that your license is eligible (type, age, bundle).
3. Tell the buyer the fee and the expected delay before they pay.
4. Once paid, uninstall the plugin and deactivate it on every computer and iLok.
5. Start the transfer with the buyer's exact name, email or iLok User ID. Double-check them: some transfers can't be undone.
6. Keep every confirmation email until the buyer has the license.

## Checklist for buyers

1. Create an account with the developer (and iLok, if needed) before you buy.
2. Check the developer's rules yourself, especially if the transfer fee is on you.
3. Pay with a protected method. On Plugin Resale, that's PayPal Goods & Services ([why it matters](/blog/how-to-buy-used-plugins-safely)).
4. Don't confirm you've received the license until it shows up in **your own** account.
5. Install, authorize and test it.
