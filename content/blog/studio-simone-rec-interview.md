Samy Gérard, also known as Sam Sam, runs Studio Simone Rec., a small studio room in Paris where he has been mixing, composing for picture and producing music for almost ten years. Before that, he spent seven years as a land surveyor in the south of France, DJing at parties on weekends.

We talked about that career switch, his hybrid mix chain, a 1979 French console he keeps just for the fun of recording, and the plugins he always comes back to.

## You and the studio

### Introduce yourself in a few lines: who you are, and what you do at Studio Simone Rec.

I'm Samy Gérard, or Sam Sam (aka Studio Simone when I DJ at parties, that's where the studio's name comes from). I'm 39, and I'm a sound engineer, a composer for picture and a music producer. I've been set up in my room in Paris for almost ten years. It's a fairly modest room, but it lets me work on a lot of different projects.

### You started out in electronic music in 2004. How did you get from there to sound engineering?

I had another life before! I was a land surveyor in the south of France for seven years, with a lot of parties on the side as a DJ at weekends. DJing is clearly what led me to composing, and then to software like Ableton. The stories of how songs and albums get made always fascinated me, on top of being a geek.

I looked into it and found a sound engineering school in Toulouse, [ISPRA](https://ispra.fr/). Right after school I moved to Paris. I couldn't find an internship or a job, so I quickly found my own room, without really overthinking it.

### Studio Simone Rec. is a hybrid studio. In practice, what's analog, what stays in the computer, and why?

My mix chain is pretty consistent, because of how much recall today's market demands (by the way, I highly recommend an app called [Session Recall](https://www.session-recall.com/en/), it's amazing for recalling outboard gear). I mix in the box in Pro Tools with plugins, then go out to analog into a [Vintage Maker](https://vintagemaker.net/fr-sommateur-analogique/) Neumann V475-2c summing mixer with two inserts (EQ and compressor), then back into the computer through a Mytek ADC96. Simple and effective.

I'm also lucky enough to have an analog console by the French brand Saje. It's an Auxy 26/8/4 from 1979 that came from the Sartrouville theatre. It's only there for the joy of recording! Way too big, but I don't care, haha. I can mix on it, just for me, on compositions for projects close to my heart. The preamps have an API kind of colour (Saje fans actually call it "the poor man's API"), and there's a 3-band parametric EQ that I love, on the 8 aux channels too.

### You work on pop, rap and electronic music, but also sound design and sonic branding. How do you move from one world to another?

I think it comes from my background and my story. When I started ten years ago, I took on a lot of projects in every direction, and that taught me a lot. Especially mixing short films, which are often missing music, so you find a way in as a composer too. Then on the music side, you get your friends' tracks placed in syncs, and so on.

I'm convinced these two worlds live side by side and need each other. Now that I've built my business this way, I'd have a really hard time doing only music mixing, or only post-production for picture, or only music production.

### A recent project or artist you're particularly proud of, and why?

All of them. Especially the ones I'm working on right now, because I'm still learning on them.

In the end, I don't have an artist profile in the classic sense. I'd probably have a hard time defending a project on stage, or a film in competition. I have a huge amount of respect for people who do that, who carry their work and their vision all the way. So when someone trusts me to put my touch on their project, I feel it very strongly. There's something really energising about someone handing you their world and letting you bring something personal to it. That's probably what makes me proudest.

OK, let's still do a bit of name-dropping: [Jon Onj](https://jononj.love/), who has clearly been part of the furniture at Studio Simone Rec. since the start. And on the picture side, a young director, [Bartolomé Chapel](https://allstudiosimone.com/RADE_REF). Between us, it just clicks.

## Plugins

### Your main DAW, and why that one?

Ableton Live for composing, Pro Tools for mixing and post-production.

### The 3 to 5 plugins you couldn't work without?

- **FabFilter Pro-Q 4**
- **UAD Pultec EQ**
- **FabFilter Pro-MB**
- **Waves SSL compressor** (out of habit)
- **UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B**

### An underrated plugin everyone should know?

Impossible to name just one, sorry.

- **[Soundly Place It](https://getsoundly.com/news/place-it-as-a-standalone-plugin/)**: a free post-production plugin that places any element or source in an environment in seconds, especially with its presets: kitchen, phone, gardens, inside a car, outside between two buildings... Incredible!
- **[Black Salt Audio Clipper](https://blacksaltaudio.com/clipper/)**: a small, very simple clipper, very effective on individual elements and very quick to use (and that matters). Not for mastering, though.
- **[Kit Plugins BB N105](https://kitplugins.com/products/kit-bb-n105)**: an EQ modelled on a Neve 8078, the one at Blackbird Studio in the US. Pure pleasure.

### A plugin you bought and (almost) never use?

None. When I buy, it's because I'm sure ;) I test them a lot on free trials first, and I take the plunge on Black Friday! That said, it's not impossible that my Native Instruments bundle license shows up in a listing on pluginresale.com soon.

### Have you ever bought or resold a second-hand plugin license? How did it go?

Yes, a Traktor, a long time ago. It was a pain.

### What's on your master bus (or mix bus), roughly?

Always a tape plugin (whichever one, I always find what I need). Soothe, which does a good job on the resonances that come from summing. Sometimes a multiband to fix a bit of dynamics.

But the important thing is to also leave room for the mastering engineer. Either because they have gear I don't have to add some colour, or because they handle loudness much better than I do.

## To wrap up

### One piece of advice for someone building a home studio on a small plugin budget?

Stock plugins: very underrated and not sexy, but they work. There are also lots of incredible free plugins. And wait for Black Friday...

## Samy's plugins, second-hand

Each developer has its own rules for reselling a license. Here is how it works for the plugins Samy mentions:

- **[FabFilter Pro-Q 4](/browse?q=Pro-Q%204)** and **[Pro-MB](/browse?q=Pro-MB)**: FabFilter charges about €10 for a transfer, bought by the seller in their FabFilter account. Bundles can only be transferred as a whole. [FabFilter transfer rules](/developers/fabfilter).
- **[Waves SSL G-Master Buss Compressor](/browse?q=SSL%20G-Master)**: Waves charges 5% of the product's list price (min $10, max $150), paid by the seller, and the license needs an active Waves Update Plan. Details in our [Waves transfer guide](/blog/how-to-transfer-a-waves-license).
- **[soothe2](/browse?q=soothe2)**: the seller buys a license transfer on oeksound.com, and the license has to be at least 30 days old. [oeksound transfer rules](/developers/oeksound).
- **UAD Pultec and Tube-Tech CL 1B**: UAD plug-ins can't be sold on their own. They only change hands together with the Apollo or UAD-2 hardware they're registered to. [Universal Audio transfer rules](/developers/universal-audio). Softube's native [Tube-Tech CL 1B](/browse?q=Tube-Tech%20CL%201B), on the other hand, transfers through iLok ($25 fee).
- **Native Instruments bundles, and [Traktor Pro](/browse?q=Traktor%20Pro)**: NI licenses move with a Transfer ID created in the seller's NI account, which the buyer then imports. See our [Native Instruments transfer guide](/blog/how-to-transfer-a-native-instruments-license).
