Omnisphere is one of those instruments almost every producer has owned at some point. Keyscape and Trilian aren't far behind. They're also expensive, which makes a used license attractive for buyers, and worth selling if it's gathering dust.

Spectrasonics is stricter than most developers about transfers, though. Here's what's allowed, what isn't, and how to do it right.

## Can you resell a Spectrasonics license?

Yes, but with tight rules. Spectrasonics points out that most sample-based instruments on the market don't allow transfers at all, and that it makes an exception for legitimate cases. The conditions:

- **Only the original purchaser** can request a transfer.
- **Once per license.** A license you bought second-hand can't be resold again.
- **Each request is reviewed case by case**, and approval isn't guaranteed.

The instruments are what can be transferred: Omnisphere, Keyscape, Trilian and Stylus RMX. Spectrasonics says **license transfers aren't available for its sound library products**.

## What about Sonic Extensions and upgrades?

- **Omnisphere Sonic Extensions** you own can be transferred **together with an approved Omnisphere transfer**. They don't go on their own.
- If you used a **special upgrade or crossgrade program**, you can't resell the original license you upgraded from.
- If you own a **used (transferred) license**, you can't resell the upgraded license either.

## How much does it cost?

When a transfer is granted, Spectrasonics says there's often an additional fee of **$50 per instrument**, covering the transfer of all rights to the new user. In our database, the fee is **usually paid by the seller**, but buyer and seller can agree otherwise. Settle it before the deal.

## How to transfer a Spectrasonics license, step by step

1. **Check that you're eligible:** you're the original purchaser, the license was never transferred, and it's an instrument, not a sound library.
2. **Agree on the deal with the buyer**, including who pays the $50 fee. Tell the buyer the transfer takes weeks and needs approval.
3. **Send a written request** to Spectrasonics at info@spectrasonics.net, as the original owner.
4. **Wait for the review.** Spectrasonics says processing takes **a few weeks**.
5. **Once approved**, the new owner gets a new serial number and account.

Because approval isn't guaranteed, think about the order of operations with your buyer. On Plugin Resale, the buyer pays with PayPal Goods & Services, which keeps them protected if the transfer falls through.

## Traps to avoid

**Selling a license you bought used.** It can only be transferred once, by the original purchaser. If you're not that person, it can't be resold.

**Selling a sound library.** Spectrasonics doesn't transfer its sound library products.

**Promising a quick delivery.** A few weeks is normal. A buyer who expects the license the same day will be frustrated. Say it clearly in your listing.

**Splitting Sonic Extensions from Omnisphere.** They only go with the Omnisphere license.

## How to price a used Spectrasonics instrument

Start from what the buyer would pay for it new today. Then remember what makes a used Spectrasonics license less convenient than a new one: a wait of a few weeks, an approval that isn't guaranteed, and a license the buyer will never be able to resell. That's worth a real discount.

Then adjust:

- **Factor in the $50 fee** and who pays it.
- **Mention any Sonic Extensions** that come with an Omnisphere license: they add value.
