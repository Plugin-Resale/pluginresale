You've found out that your developer allows license transfers. Great, but that doesn't mean every license from that developer can be resold. The way you got the license matters just as much as who made it.

Here are the license types that usually can't be transferred, with real examples from the policies in our [transfer rules database](/developers).

## NFR licenses (Not For Resale)

NFR licenses are given away for free: to reviewers, beta testers, artists, dealers, or at events and giveaways. As the name says, they're not meant to be sold, and almost every developer that has a transfer policy excludes them explicitly. In our database, **32 developers** that allow transfers specifically mention that NFR licenses can't be moved.

If you got a plugin for free from the developer and not in a public sale, assume it's NFR.

## Educational licenses

Student and teacher licenses are sold at a big discount, on the condition that they're used for education. Most developers don't let you resell them at all: Native Instruments, iZotope, FabFilter and Steinberg all exclude them.

A few developers allow it under conditions. **Ableton** lets you transfer an educational license only to another eligible student or teacher, and **Bitwig** allows it if the new owner proves they're eligible.

## Licenses bundled with hardware (OEM)

Many audio interfaces, controllers and keyboards come with free software: a "lite" DAW, a few plugins, a sound library. These OEM licenses are tied to the hardware and usually can't be sold on their own.

- **Steinberg**: OEM versions of Cubase (AI, LE, and Elements when it's bundled with hardware) can't be sold separately from the hardware.
- **XLN Audio**: licenses bundled with Focusrite, Novation or Cakewalk products can't be transferred.
- **Universal Audio**: UAD plug-ins can only be sold together with the Apollo, Satellite or UAD-2 unit they're registered to.

If you sell the hardware, the bundled software often goes with it.

## Subscriptions and rent-to-own

A subscription gives you access for as long as you pay, so there's nothing to sell. Rent-to-own plans are the same until they're fully paid. Examples of subscriptions that can't be transferred: **Waves Creative Access**, **Slate Digital All Access** and **Output Arcade**. **AIR Music Technology** excludes rent-to-own licenses too.

EU law on software resale also only covers licenses bought outright for an unlimited time. We explain it in [Can I legally resell my audio plugins?](/blog/can-i-legally-resell-my-plugins)

## Splitting a bundle

Bought a big bundle and only use three plugins from it? You'd probably like to sell the rest separately. Many developers don't allow it: **Native Instruments**, **FabFilter**, **D16 Group** and **AIR** only let you transfer a bundle as a whole.

Some developers are more flexible: **Kilohearts**, for example, transfers bundle licenses one by one. Check your developer's page before you list individual plugins from a bundle.

## Upgrades and crossgrades

An upgrade is a discount on a new version for people who already own the old one. That creates a chain: the new license depends on the old one.

- **Avid**: a license already used to upgrade can't be transferred separately from the upgraded version.
- **MOTU**: once you've upgraded, you can't sell the previous version on its own.
- **XLN Audio**: upgraded Addictive Drums 1 and 2 licenses have to be sold together.
- **iZotope**: only the current version is transferred, and past versions are disabled.
- **Bitwig**: crossgrade licenses can be transferred only if the new owner is eligible for the crossgrade.

In short: you sell the latest version, and you give up the older ones with it.

## Licenses that were already transferred

Some developers limit how many times a license can change hands. **iZotope** doesn't allow a second transfer of the same product, and **Sample Modeling** only lets the original owner resell, once. Others charge more for a second transfer: **Arturia** is free for first-hand licenses but charges €10 if the license was itself bought second-hand.

## Very recent licenses

Many developers make you wait before you can transfer a license you just bought: 30 days for Toontrack and oeksound, 60 days for Native Instruments, 90 days for Softube, Sonnox and anything moved through iLok, 6 months for Arturia and Acustica Audio.

## How to check your license

1. **Look at your account** on the developer's website: the license type (full, NFR, EDU, upgrade…) is often shown next to the product.
2. **Check your original order** or the email with your serial number.
3. **Look up the developer** in our [transfer rules database](/developers), which lists each developer's restrictions with a link to the official source.

When you [list a plugin on Plugin Resale](/sell), you confirm that you own a full license that isn't NFR, educational or bundled. It saves everyone time, and it protects buyers from paying for a license that can't be moved.
