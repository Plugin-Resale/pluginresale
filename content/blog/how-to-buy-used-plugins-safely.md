Buying a plugin second-hand is one of the best ways to build a serious toolkit on a budget. It's also a place where a few people try their luck, because a license is invisible: there's no box to ship, just a name that has to change in a database.

Most scams follow the same few patterns. Once you know them, they're easy to avoid.

## Rule number one: pay with PayPal Goods & Services

On Plugin Resale, buyers pay sellers directly with **PayPal Goods & Services**. It's the payment type meant for buying something, and it gives you access to PayPal's buyer protection if the seller never delivers (subject to PayPal's own conditions).

**Never agree to pay with Friends & Family.** That option is meant for sending money to people you know, and it gives you no protection if something goes wrong. A seller who insists on it, or offers a "discount" to avoid PayPal's fees, is a big red flag.

The same goes for bank transfers, gift cards, crypto or payment apps a stranger suggests: if the license never arrives, you have no way to get your money back.

## Red flags

- **The seller wants to move the conversation elsewhere** (WhatsApp, email, Discord) before you've even agreed on anything.
- **The price is far too good.** A €300 plugin for €40 is usually an NFR copy, a cracked version, a shared account or nothing at all.
- **The seller offers to send you their login** instead of transferring the license. You'd be using an account you don't own, which is against almost every license agreement and can be shut down at any time.
- **The seller only sends a serial number** for a product that requires an official transfer. The license stays registered to them, and they could still use or revoke it.
- **The seller can't tell you how the transfer works** for their developer, or says the developer "doesn't need to know".
- **Pressure to hurry**: "someone else wants it, pay now".

## Check the license before you pay

Ask the seller, or check yourself:

1. **Can this developer's licenses be transferred?** Look it up in our [transfer rules database](/developers). Some well-known developers don't allow resale at all.
2. **What kind of license is it?** NFR, educational and hardware-bundled licenses usually can't be resold ([here's why](/blog/nfr-educational-bundled-licenses-resale)).
3. **Is there a transfer fee, and who pays it?** Agree on it before paying, so the final price holds no surprise.
4. **Which version is it?** Some developers only transfer the latest version, or require an active update plan.

## Don't confirm too early

The deal is only done when the license shows up **in your own account** (developer account or iLok) and the plugin authorizes on your computer. Until then:

- Don't mark the license as received.
- Keep all messages and emails, including the developer's confirmation.
- If the seller goes quiet after being paid, message them through the site first, then open a case with PayPal while you're still within PayPal's time limit.

## How a purchase works on Plugin Resale

1. You click **Buy with PayPal** on a listing. The listing is reserved for you.
2. You see the seller's PayPal email and pay with **Goods & Services**, with the listing ID in the note.
3. The seller confirms the payment and starts the transfer with the developer.
4. You confirm once the license is in your account. The deal is complete, and you can both leave a review.

The seller's PayPal email is never public: only the buyer of an active deal can see it. Messages stay on the site, next to the listing. And every seller has a public profile with their past sales and reviews, so you can see who you're dealing with.
